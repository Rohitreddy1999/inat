# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

People who keep starting things but don't — the ones who keep saying "when life calms down, when I'm ready, when the time is right." Four life stages: students still figuring out identity, career builders who've stalled on a personal goal, parents running on empty with no time for themselves, reinventors who've already changed once and know they can again. They don't need inspiration; they need a clear path on a specific morning when they wake up and actually do something.

The person who opens this app on Day 1 and the person who closes it on Day 21 are meaningfully different. That transformation is the product.

## Product Purpose

INAT gives users a 21-day structured path in one of five areas — Move, Rhythm, Express, Calm, Mindful — so they feel confident enough to continue independently by Day 22. It is not a tracker, a streak machine, or a self-help library. It is a base: one decision, 21 days, then it hands them back to themselves. By Day 22, INAT gave them the start. What they do with it is theirs.

## Positioning

The starting point that ends on purpose — so you leave capable, not dependent.

## Brand Personality

Fierce and earned. Defiant underdog. The app carries Novak Djokovic's INAT spirit: relentless refusal to accept theoretical limits. Not a cheerleader, not a therapist. The energy of a coach who has already decided you can do it and shows up to verify that. The design finds flow through system — no inspiration required, just the structure that makes showing up feel inevitable.

Three words: **earned**, **electric**, **certain**.

The voice is terse and true. "The circuit doesn't judge. It waits." Sentences that feel like they already know you'll be back.

## Anti-references

**Wellness and meditation apps**: no Headspace beige, no soft pastels, no breathing-room whitespace as a substitute for meaning. The dark palette is load-bearing — it signals that this is serious and intentional.

**Corporate productivity tools**: INAT is not a task manager or habit tracker wearing a journey costume. No Notion neutrals, no Linear precision-grid aesthetic. No checkbox energy.

**Motivational poster energy**: no mountain photography, no "you got this" copy, no abstract uplift. The product earns every encouraging word by not saying it unless it's true. "You're building the base. Show up." — not "Believe in yourself."

## Design Principles

**Show up, not feel inspired.** The product asks for action. Motivation is not the currency — structure is. Every screen reduces friction and increases signal.

**Earned, not granted.** Completion carries weight because it requires a real hold. Day unlock is a calendar gate, not a timer. The graduation moment matters because the 21 days actually happened.

**The circuit doesn't judge — it waits.** No penalties, no resets, no streak pressure. A user who returns after two weeks sees the same day waiting, not a shame screen. This is kindness designed as confidence.

**Flow through system.** The app creates the conditions for flow, not the experience of flow itself. By Day 22 the user knows how to create that system themselves. Dependency would be a design failure.

**Day 22 is the real product.** Every interaction is in service of a user who doesn't need the app anymore. The Graduation screen exists to close the loop cleanly, then let go.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the baseline. The dark palette requires special care: all text must meet 4.5:1 against Abyss (#07090D) and Fathom (#0F141A) backgrounds. Arc-Light (#EAFFF5) on Abyss is the primary text combination. Phase colors (Surge, Glacial, Plasma) are accent-only — never used as the sole carrier of information. Reduced motion support required for all Reanimated 3 animations. No assumptions about hand dominance in tap targets (HoldButton centered, minimum 48×48pt touch targets throughout).
